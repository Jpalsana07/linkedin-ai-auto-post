We chose SSR.

Our Next.js app had one job: load fast for first-time visitors and still feel alive after that.

That meant deciding where the page should be built.

With Next.js, you can render pages on the server before they hit the browser.
Or you can ship more JavaScript and let the browser do the work.

The tradeoff is simple.
SSR (server-side rendering, HTML made on the server) gives you faster first paint and better SEO.
Client-heavy pages feel snappier after load, but they often ship a bigger bundle and wait longer before anything useful appears.

We chose SSR for the homepage and product pages.

Why?

Because our biggest constraint wasn’t developer speed.
It was a 2.1 second median load time on mobile, and half our traffic came from people on flaky connections.

That constraint changed the decision.

A prettier app that loads late still loses the sale.

So we accepted a little more server complexity to keep the first screen fast.

That tradeoff does not always apply.
If your app is mostly behind login, or your users live inside it for hours, client-side rendering can be the better call.

The lesson: optimize for the moment the user cares most about.

What do you bias toward in Next.js: faster first paint or richer client-side behavior?
