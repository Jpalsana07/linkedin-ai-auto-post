We almost split the app.

I was shipping a React product where the homepage and dashboard lived in the same codebase.
It felt tidy.
It also meant one small change could break the login flow.

The decision was simple: keep one React app, or split it into separate apps.
In plain English, it was about whether every page should share the same suitcase, or pack its own.

The tradeoff was speed versus safety.
One app is faster to move early, because shared components and routing are easy.
Separate apps are safer later, because marketing can ship without touching product code.

We chose one app.

Why?
Because the real constraint was not code size.
It was team size.
We had 3 engineers, and splitting early would have doubled our release paths, our test surface, and our chance of shipping a broken redirect.

That mattered more than elegance.

Six months later, the app had grown enough that this choice started to hurt.
But by then we had the users, the patterns, and the proof to justify the split.

The lesson: pick the structure that matches your team today, not the architecture you hope to deserve.
