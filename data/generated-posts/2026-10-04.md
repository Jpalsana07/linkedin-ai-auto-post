Our eval passed. It still failed.

We were shipping an AI support assistant for a customer team.
The goal was simple: answer tickets faster without making the humans clean up bad replies.

In staging, the bot looked great.
We ran 200 test prompts and got a 92% pass rate.

Then real customers asked messy things.
One user wrote, “I got charged twice, but only one charge shows up.”

The bot answered with a refund policy.
Technically correct.

Completely useless.

Our eval had measured whether the answer sounded right.
It had not measured whether the answer solved the customer’s actual problem.

That mistake cost us a week and a lot of trust.

We rebuilt the eval around outcomes, not style.
Instead of “Did it sound helpful?” we asked “Did it ask the right follow-up, route the issue, or resolve it?”

The lesson is simple:
AI evals only matter when they match the real job.

A pretty score on a test set can hide a broken product.
