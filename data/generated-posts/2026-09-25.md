Topic: PostgreSQL Lessons

Date: 2026-09-25

---

Postgres can be too helpful.

We were building a product where users could search millions of rows and get results back fast.

The question was simple: do we let PostgreSQL do the heavy lifting, or add a separate search system?

PostgreSQL is the database that stores the data and answers questions about it.
A search system is a second index built just for finding things quickly.

The tradeoff is speed versus simplicity.

Postgres is easier to operate.
One backup, one permissions model, one place to look when something breaks.

A separate search engine can feel faster at larger scale.
But it adds another moving part, another bill, and another failure mode.

We stayed with PostgreSQL and used indexed queries first.
That meant a few searches were not as flashy as a dedicated engine, but the product stayed simpler to run.

The constraint most people miss is team size.

If a 3-person team has to support 2 databases at 2 a.m., the “better” architecture starts looking expensive.
For us, boring won.

This tradeoff does not hold forever.
If search becomes the product, I’d split it out sooner.

The lesson: don’t add a second system until the pain is bigger than the complexity.

When have you kept everything in PostgreSQL instead of splitting the stack?
