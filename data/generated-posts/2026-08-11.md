# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Career Inflection Moments

Date: 2026-08-11

---

I missed the promotion.

We were rebuilding our internal review process so 40 people could move faster.
My job was simple: make career decisions feel fair, not random.

I thought I had enough data.
Peer feedback, manager notes, self reviews.

Then the VP asked why one engineer had been passed over twice.
I pulled up the packet and realized the problem.

The strongest work was buried in 14 pages of context.
The actual decision was hidden in a paragraph nobody read.

We had built a process that looked thorough and still failed the person it was meant to help.

I cut the packet to one page.
Three signals only.
What changed, what was hard, what impact it had.

The next review cycle took 20 minutes instead of an hour.
And managers finally stopped arguing about formatting and started arguing about evidence.

The lesson: if a career process is too hard to read, it is too hard to trust.

What’s the most broken career process you’ve seen, and what made it fail?
