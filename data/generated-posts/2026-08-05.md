# LinkedIn Post

Topic: LLM in Production

Date: 2026-08-05

---

We shipped a bad answer.

I was rolling out an LLM to write support replies for a product used by 20,000 customers.
The goal was simple: cut first-response time from 12 minutes to under 2, without making people feel ignored.

The model looked great in tests.
Then it hit real tickets.

It kept sounding confident on refund cases, even when it was wrong.
One reply told a customer to wait 7 days for a payment that had already cleared.
That one mistake turned a 30-second draft into a 3-email escalation.

Our problem was not the model.
It was that we treated a language model like a finished product instead of a draft with a memory problem.

We had to add a human review step for any message touching money, deadlines, or account access.
After that, the bot could handle the easy 80%, and people only touched the risky 20%.

The lesson: in production, speed is useless if the system can’t know when it should stop talking.

What kind of AI mistake do you think deserves a human by default?
