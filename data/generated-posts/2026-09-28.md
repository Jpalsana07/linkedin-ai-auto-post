A microservice blew up.

We were shipping a new checkout flow for a client with 2 million monthly visits.
The goal was simple: split one slow backend into smaller services so the site could handle more traffic.

Instead, the first release made everything slower.

Every page load now had to call 6 services.
One request turned into a tiny road trip.

The worst part was that each service looked clean on its own.
But the system was paying the cost of all those handoffs:
more network hops, more failure points, more waiting.

We spent a week tracing logs in Datadog, the monitoring tool, and watching latency climb from 180ms to 640ms.

The fix was embarrassingly simple.
We merged three of the services back together and kept the split only where the boundaries were actually real.

The lesson: clean code is not the same thing as a good architecture.
If the pieces talk to each other constantly, they are probably not separate enough.

What architecture mistake taught you that boundaries have to earn their existence?
