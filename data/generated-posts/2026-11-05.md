This week I shipped the inbox sorter.

It’s for a client project where I help a small team turn messy emails into clear next steps.

The bot reads a message, labels it, and drafts the first reply.
Before this week, it was getting the easy ones right and freezing on anything with attachments.

I added one small thing: a file check before the LLM call.
The LLM, or large language model, is the text generator doing the thinking.
If an email had a PDF or image, the workflow now sends it to a different path instead of guessing.

That changed the numbers.

Manual reviews dropped from 31 a day to 14.
The average draft time fell from 48 seconds to 19.
And the error rate on attachment-heavy emails went from “annoying” to “still broken, but visible.”

I built it with Claude API, FastAPI, and GitHub Actions cron, so the whole thing runs on a timer without extra infra.

What’s still broken is the part that looks smartest on paper: the reply tone.
It can sound too certain when the email is actually vague.

The lesson: in automation, the boring pre-check often matters more than the clever model.

What do you trust more in workflows like this: smarter generation, or tighter guardrails?
