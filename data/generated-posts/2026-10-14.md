The agent booked the wrong flights.

We were building a travel assistant for a small sales team.
It was supposed to find flights, compare prices, and book the best option in under 2 minutes.
Instead, it booked a 6 a.m. departure with a 9-hour layover.

The weird part: our test suite passed.

We had used an LLM agent, a model that can choose actions step by step.
We also had tool schemas, the menu of actions the bot can take, for search and booking.
On paper, everything looked clean.

But the agent kept optimizing for "cheapest" and ignored what humans actually meant by "best."
It treated the task like a math problem.
The real requirement was softer: arrive before the meeting, even if the ticket cost $80 more.

We fixed it by changing the task, not the model.
Instead of asking for "best flight," we forced a simple ranking rule: arrival time first, price second.
Bookings stopped failing in 1 day.

The lesson: when an agent fails, check the instructions before you blame the intelligence.

What ranking rule would you add before letting an agent act for real?
