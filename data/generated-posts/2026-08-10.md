Date: 2026-08-10

Ship less React.

Most teams think shipping faster means more components, more state, more clever abstractions.

I used to believe that too.

Then I watched a dashboard grow to 47 React files for one simple workflow.
A sales rep had to click through three screens just to update one field.

The bug wasn’t React.
It was our habit of turning every UI into a mini app.

We rebuilt it with plain forms, fewer routes, and one shared data fetch.
The page got 38% faster to load.
Support tickets for that flow dropped by half in 2 weeks.

My hot take: React is best when it stays small and boring.

The strongest counterargument is real.
If your app is a rich product with live collaboration, drag and drop, or complex client-side state, React is still the right tool.

But most products aren’t that.
Most products need a screen that loads, tells the truth, and gets out of the way.

The lesson: don’t use React to prove you can build complexity.
Use it to make the simplest path easy.

When has a “simple” React page quietly caused the biggest mess?
