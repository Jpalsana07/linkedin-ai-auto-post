One table, or three?

We were building a billing system for a small SaaS.
Every subscription, refund, and failed payment had to be recorded correctly.
If we got it wrong, we’d lose money or send the wrong email.

The decision was simple on paper:
keep everything in one PostgreSQL table, or split it into separate tables for payments, invoices, and events.

One table is easier to query.
You can see the whole story in one place.
Three tables feel cleaner at first.
Each one has fewer columns, and each team can own its own piece.

We chose one table.

Not because it was elegant.
Because the hidden cost was joins at read time.
Our support team needed answers in under 2 seconds, and they were searching by customer email, date, and payment state all day.
With three tables, every lookup became a tiny scavenger hunt.

The lesson:
pick the shape that matches how people will search, not how tidy it looks in a diagram.

That tradeoff changes if the data gets huge, or if each part has a truly different life cycle.
Then splitting can be worth it.

What would you optimize for first: cleaner tables or faster answers?
