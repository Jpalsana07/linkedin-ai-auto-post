Microservices were a mistake.

The popular story says small services are cleaner.
Each team owns its part.
Each deploy is safer.
Each bug stays small.

We believed that too.

Then a checkout flow that should have been one simple request turned into 14 network calls across 6 services.
A tiny change took 9 days because three teams had to coordinate deployments.

We spent more time tracing edges than building features.
The architecture looked elegant on slides.
In production, it felt like debugging with a blindfold on.

So we collapsed it into one service and kept the boundaries inside the codebase.
Deploys dropped from 6 to 1.
The team shipped the next payment change in 2 days.

The strongest argument for microservices is real:
they help when teams are truly independent and the system is already at a huge scale.

But most products are not there.
Most teams need speed, not ceremony.

My lesson: start with the simplest shape that lets you move fast, then split only when the pain is obvious.
