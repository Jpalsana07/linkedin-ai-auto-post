# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Startup Lessons

Date: 2026-09-26

---

We built the wrong startup.

It was a recruiting tool for small teams.
I thought speed was the product.
It wasn't.

We shipped the first version in 6 weeks.
It had a clean dashboard, one-click outreach, and a workflow that looked great in demos.

Nobody stuck around.

We kept blaming the funnel.
More ads.
Better copy.
Another landing page.

The real problem showed up in customer calls.
Recruiters didn't need another place to send messages.
They needed proof that a candidate was actually worth pursuing.

We had built a motion machine.
They needed a decision machine.

That was the postmortem:
our product solved the visible task, not the expensive judgment.

Once we saw that, the whole plan changed.
We cut features, narrowed the buyer, and rebuilt around candidate scoring instead of sending more emails.

The lesson: if your product saves time but not doubt, people may try it and still not trust it.

What product have you seen fail because it made work faster, but not clearer?
