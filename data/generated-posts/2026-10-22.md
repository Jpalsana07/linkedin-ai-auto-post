Next.js is not your bottleneck.

The hot take:
your app is usually slower because of product decisions, not the framework.

The common story is simple.
If the site feels slow, people blame Next.js and start rewriting everything.
I used to do that too.

Then I shipped a dashboard on Next.js 14 with 8 routes, server components, and a Stripe checkout.
The page felt “slow” in review.
The real problem was 11 images loading at once and a 1.8 MB analytics script hiding in the background.

We fixed those two things.
No framework swap.
No rebuild.
Just smaller images, fewer client-side widgets, and moving the heavy script off the critical path.

Load time dropped from 4.9 seconds to 2.1 seconds on a mid-range laptop.

The strongest counterargument is real:
Next.js can get messy when teams mix server and client code carelessly.
That’s true.

But messy code is still a team problem.
Not a reason to blame the framework first.

My lesson:
most “Next.js problems” are really product and loading problems wearing a framework costume.
