The camera was the easy part.
The hard part was memory.

I was building a vision feature that had to look at uploaded images and extract one small detail reliably.
Not a demo. A real step in a production flow.

The choice was simple on paper:
send one full image to a vision-language model, or cut the image into smaller pieces first.

Full images keep the whole scene.
That matters when the clue depends on context.
But they also cost more, take longer, and sometimes miss tiny details.

So I tried both.
For wide shots, I used a VLM first, then cropped only when the answer looked uncertain.
For close-up tasks, I skipped the full pass and sent only the crop.

That saved about 40% of the API work on one flow, and the answers got steadier.
The constraint most people miss is not accuracy.
It’s cost per retry.

If the model is wrong, you don’t just pay once.
You pay again to verify it, and again to recover from the bad result.

So I chose the smaller input when the task was local and visual.
I kept the full frame only when context could change the answer.

This tradeoff does not hold when the scene itself matters more than the object.
A tiny crop can be perfect and still be useless.

When do you keep the whole image instead of cropping first?
