# LinkedIn Post

Topic: AI Evals

Date: 2026-07-29

---

Evals are overrated.

Most teams think they need a perfect scorecard before they ship.
A giant spreadsheet. A thousand labeled examples. A scoreboard nobody trusts.

I used to think that too.

Then I watched a small support bot go live with 27 hand-written eval cases in LangSmith, the test dashboard for AI apps.
Not 2,000.
27.

We were trying to catch one ugly failure: the bot confidently giving refunds for orders it never saw.
The first eval set missed it.
The second one, built from real customer chats, caught it in 20 minutes.

That changed how I think about evals.

The best eval is not the most complete one.
It is the one that matches the failure you actually fear.

Yes, a stronger argument exists: without broad coverage, you can miss weird edge cases and fool yourself with a tiny test set.
That is real.
But if your evals are too abstract to reflect a real user mistake, they become theater.

The lesson: start with the sharpest failure, not the prettiest framework.

What failure would you build your first eval around?
