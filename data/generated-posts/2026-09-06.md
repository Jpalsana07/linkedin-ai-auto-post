# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Startup Lessons

Date: 2026-09-06

---

This week, we shipped the thing.

We’re building a startup ops tool for small founders who live in Slack, spreadsheets, and sticky notes.
This week it finally started turning messy updates into a clean weekly report.

The first version took 14 minutes to run.
Now it finishes in 38 seconds.

That mattered because people were dropping off before they saw the output.

We shipped three things:
a simpler upload flow,
a cleaner report template,
and a retry path when the data import fails halfway through.

The upload flow cut first-time setup from 11 steps to 5.
Signups didn’t jump.
But completed setups went from 27% to 51%.

What’s broken?
The report still looks wrong when a founder has duplicate project names.
One customer called it “almost right, but annoying.”
That stung, because they were correct.

Build-in-public sounds nice until the public sees the broken parts.
That’s still the point.

If you’re sharing real shipping updates, show the time saved, the conversion moved, and the thing that still feels embarrassing.

What do you think matters more in public updates: showing momentum or showing the bugs?
