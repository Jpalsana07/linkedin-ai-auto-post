Topic: AI Evals

Date: 2026-10-18

We compared two eval tools.

They both score AI outputs, so you can catch bad answers before users do.

I was building a support bot and needed to know: which responses were actually good, and which just sounded confident?

I tried OpenAI Evals and LangSmith evals.

OpenAI Evals gave us a clean way to run repeatable tests.
Great for prompts, datasets, and quick regression checks.

LangSmith evals, from LangChain’s tracing tool, made debugging easier.
It showed the full path: prompt, retrieved docs, model output, and scores.

That mattered when one answer looked fine until we saw it pulled the wrong policy page.

Setup time told the story.

OpenAI Evals took us about 2 hours to wire up.
LangSmith took closer to 30 minutes because the traces were already there.

But the winner for our team was OpenAI Evals.

Why?

Because we needed a simple gate in CI, not a pretty dashboard.
One score per test case was enough to stop bad prompts from shipping.

The lesson: pick the eval tool that matches your workflow, not the one with the fanciest UI.
