Date: 2026-10-11

I chose the wrong interview tool.

We were hiring for a product role, and the real problem was simple:
could this person explain messy work clearly, under pressure?

I compared two approaches.

The first was structured interviews.
Same questions, same scoring sheet, 45 minutes each.
It took 2 weeks to train the team.

The second was a take-home case.
Candidates got 24 hours and sent back a doc.
It felt flexible, but it quietly rewarded people with more free time.

After 18 interviews, the pattern was obvious.
The take-home produced prettier answers.
The structured interview produced better hires.

One candidate wrote a polished strategy deck in 6 hours.
In the live interview, they couldn’t defend a single decision.
Another candidate was rough on paper, but thought out loud like a real operator.

We switched to structured interviews only.

The lesson: if you want to predict real work, test in the conditions the job actually happens in.
