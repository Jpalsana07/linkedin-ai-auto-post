$200, or 20 hours?

I was choosing how to price a freelance project for a small client.
The real problem was simple: I needed a clean number before the call ended.

I tested two ways to do it.

Google Sheets was my old method.
Fast, free, and honest.
I could build a quote in 15 minutes, but I still had to remember every line item, tax, and revision rule myself.

Harvest Forecast was the other option.
A scheduling tool that turns hours into money.
It took me 2 hours to set up, but it showed me projected revenue, capacity, and what one extra week of work would cost.

For one project, Sheets underpriced me by $1,800.
Not because I was bad at math.
Because it made the easy thing visible and the expensive thing invisible.

Harvest Forecast won.

My lesson: when money is involved, the better tool is the one that makes the hidden cost obvious.
