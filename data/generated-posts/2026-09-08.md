PostgreSQL almost took us down.

We were building billing for a SaaS app, for customers who needed invoices to be exact.
One bad query turned a quiet Tuesday into a 3-hour outage.

The app looked fine.
The dashboard loaded.
But Postgres was stuck on a lock — a database traffic jam where one write blocks the others.

We had added a migration that touched a huge table.
Then a background job started updating rows one by one.
On a table with 18 million rows, that was enough to freeze everything.

We tried faster indexes, smaller batches, and a retry loop.
None of it helped.

The real problem was simpler:
we treated Postgres like storage, not a shared room with one door.

Once we understood that, we changed the shape of the work.
We moved heavy updates out of peak hours.
We split the migration into chunks.
We added a hard rule: anything that can lock a big table gets rehearsed in staging first.

Postgres is usually the safest tool in the stack.
But safety only holds when you respect its limits.

The lesson: boring databases still need traffic rules.

What’s the Postgres mistake you only learned after it hurt?
