# LinkedIn Post

Topic: SaaS Build-in-Public Metrics

Date: 2026-09-10

---

Our metrics were wrong.

We were building a SaaS in public, and the numbers had to do two jobs at once:
keep us honest, and show strangers enough progress to care.

So we wired up PostHog, the product analytics tool, and published a weekly growth chart.

Then I noticed something ugly.

Trial signups were up, but revenue was flat.
The chart looked healthy.
The business was not.

We had counted every new account as a win, even if the person never made it past the first screen.
That meant our “growth” post was really a vanity report with better lighting.

I spent a Friday fixing it.

We changed the public metric from signups to activated users, people who actually finished one core action.
The next week, the number dropped 38%.

It stung.

But the story got better, because it was true.
And truth built more trust than a prettier chart ever did.

The lesson: if you build in public, publish the number that would hurt to lose.

What metric would you trust most if your audience could see the raw truth?
