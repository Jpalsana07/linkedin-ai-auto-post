# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Freelancing with Numbers

Date: 2026-09-15

---

Price your work by outcome.

Everyone says freelance pricing is simple:
pick an hourly rate, track time, invoice later.

That sounds fair.
It also keeps you trapped.

I learned this after a 2-week project where I charged $4,000 for 40 hours.
The client got a launch page that brought in 17 qualified leads in the first month.
I had accidentally sold hours when I should have sold relief.

The strongest counterargument is real:
hourly billing is easy to explain, and it protects clients from overpaying.

But it also punishes speed.

The moment I switched to fixed-price packages with a clear result, the conversations changed.
Less debate about the clock.
More debate about the problem.

The lesson I keep coming back to:
if you can describe the outcome in plain English, don’t hide behind hours.

That doesn’t mean every project should be value-based.
Some work is still messy, open-ended, or impossible to scope cleanly.

But for the work you can define clearly, numbers should describe the result, not your calendar.

What’s the fairest way you’ve seen freelancers price real outcomes?
