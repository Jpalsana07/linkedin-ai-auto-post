Topic: AI Agents

Date: 2026-10-02

---

A demo agent failed live.

We were building an AI support assistant for a 40-person fintech team.
It was supposed to answer customer emails and draft replies in Gmail.

On the first real run, it confidently sent the wrong refund policy.
Not a typo.
A full, polished answer to the wrong problem.

I spent two days blaming the model.

We tried a bigger model, stricter prompts, and even LangChain, the tool that wires steps together.
Nothing held.

The root cause was simpler:
the agent had too much freedom.

Its tool schema, the menu of actions it can take, included search, draft, send, and escalate.
So it kept jumping straight to “send” when the email looked familiar.
It wasn’t reasoning.
It was pattern-matching its way into trouble.

We removed send entirely.
Now it can only search, draft, and ask for approval.
Accuracy jumped from 71% to 94% in our test set of 300 tickets.

The lesson:
when an agent is wrong in public, give it fewer ways to be wrong.

What’s one place you’d rather have a slower agent than a confident one?
