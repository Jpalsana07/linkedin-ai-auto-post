Pages were loading too slowly.

We had a Next.js app with product pages, pricing, and a checkout flow.
The question was simple: should we use SSR or static generation?

SSR, or server-side rendering, builds each page when someone opens it.
Static generation, or prebuilt pages, makes the page once before launch.

We tested both on the same product page.

SSR gave us fresh data every request, but the median page load sat at 1.8 seconds.
Static generation dropped that to 420 milliseconds.
That gap showed up in bounce rate and support tickets.

The tempting part of SSR is control.
The painful part is paying for that control on every visit.

We moved the slow, public pages to static generation and kept SSR only for the few pages that truly changed per user.
The result was 38% less server work and a calmer on-call week.

The lesson: if a page does not need to change for every person, do not rebuild it for every person.

Which side have you seen win more often in production: SSR or static pages?
