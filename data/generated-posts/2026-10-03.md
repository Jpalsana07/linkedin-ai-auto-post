# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Next.js in Production

Date: 2026-10-03

---

Next.js is not your default.

The hot take: most teams should not start production there.

The dominant story is simple.
Next.js is the “safe” choice because it gives you React, server rendering, and a path to ship fast.

That’s true for demos.

But at work, I saw a small team build a customer portal in Next.js and spend 3 weeks untangling caching, server actions, and deployment quirks before the first real customer even logged in.

The app was fine.
The stack was the drag.

We moved the same product to a plain React frontend plus a separate API, and the team spent less time guessing where a bug lived.

Next.js can still be the right call when you need SEO, one codebase, or a tiny team shipping a marketing site and app together.

But if your product is mostly logged-in flows, dashboards, or forms, the framework can become extra ceremony you pay for every day.

The lesson: choose the stack that matches the shape of the work, not the one that sounds smartest in a launch post.
