# LinkedIn Post

Topic: LLM in Production

Date: 2026-09-27

---

The model wasn’t the problem.

Everyone wanted a bigger model, a better prompt, or a fancier orchestration layer.

We were building a support assistant for a SaaS product.
The goal was simple: answer customer questions before a human had to.

Our first version used GPT-4 and looked great in demos.
In production, it was expensive, slow, and confidently wrong on the same 12 questions.

So we stopped tuning the model and traced the failure.
Most bad answers came from messy product docs, stale screenshots, and one huge knowledge base that mixed setup guides with marketing copy.

We split the content, cut retrieval time by 38%, and deleted 40% of the source material.
Accuracy jumped more than any prompt tweak had managed.

That’s the hot take:
production LLM work is usually an information problem, not a model problem.

The strongest counterargument is fair:
sometimes you really do need a better model for reasoning, coding, or long context.

But most teams reach for that fix too early.
They spend weeks chasing tokens when the real issue is that the bot is reading a junk drawer.

The lesson I keep seeing:
make the inputs boring before you make the model smarter.
