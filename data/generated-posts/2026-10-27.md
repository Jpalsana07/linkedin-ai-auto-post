Most automation should stay boring.

The internet says every workflow needs agents, memory, and a clever chain of tools.
My hot take: most of it should be one script with clear steps.

I built an AI automation pipeline for a client project that triaged emails, classified the request, and drafted a reply.
The first version used a multi-agent system.
It felt smart.
It also took 14 seconds, made weird handoffs, and failed in places that were hard to explain.

So I cut it down to Claude API plus a plain backend workflow in Node.js.
One step reads the message.
One step classifies it.
One step drafts the output.
No drama. No hidden state.

The result was less impressive on a demo, but much better in production.
Latency dropped to under 4 seconds.
More importantly, when something broke, I could point to the exact step instead of asking three agents what they “meant.”

That said, the counterargument is real.
If the task is open-ended, messy, or needs back-and-forth reasoning, agents can be worth the complexity.
I still use them there.

The lesson: start with the simplest workflow that can fail in one obvious place.

What kind of automation do you think actually deserves agents?
