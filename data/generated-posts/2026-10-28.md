A mask was off by 12 pixels.

I was building a vision flow that needed to find a person in a frame, then cut the background cleanly for a client project.
If the cut was wrong, the final image looked broken, so the whole feature depended on that one edge.

The model was SAM2, a segmentation model that draws the object boundary.
It looked great in local tests.
Then I checked a few real images and saw the mask drifting just enough to chop off hands, hair, and shoulders.

The mistake was assuming the model saw the same thing I saw.
It did not.
A tiny crop change, a busy background, or a face at the edge of the frame could shift the result by a few pixels and make the output feel wrong to a human.

I spent about 3 hours adding a second pass with CLIPSeg, a text-guided mask model, and a few plain checks before accepting the result.
That extra step caught the bad cases early and made the output stable enough to ship through FastAPI.

The lesson: with computer vision, “looks fine in the notebook” is not enough.
You need a human-shaped check before you trust the mask.

Where have you seen a model be technically correct and still look wrong to a person?
