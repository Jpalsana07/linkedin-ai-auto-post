I stopped adding more agents.

I was building an AI agent that could review code, triage email, and draft replies.
The obvious move was to split it into specialist agents, each with one job.

That sounds neat.
It also sounds like more prompts, more handoffs, and more places for Claude API calls to disagree.

So I kept one agent and gave it a small tool menu instead.
The menu of actions the bot can take was narrower, but every step was easier to test.
I could trace one path through FastAPI instead of guessing which agent forgot context.

The tradeoff is simple.
More agents can feel smarter on paper.
One agent with good tools is usually easier to debug, cheaper to run, and less likely to drift when the task is small and the workflow is fixed.

I chose the smaller system because the real constraint was not intelligence.
It was reliability under messy inputs and a human needing to understand what happened 2 minutes later.

This tradeoff breaks down when the work is open-ended, long-running, or needs separate reasoning styles.

What do you reach for first: more agents, or better tools?
