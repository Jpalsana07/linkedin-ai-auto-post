Two type systems, one bug.

We were shipping a billing dashboard in TypeScript, and one bad payload could quietly break invoices for 200 customers.

The choice was simple:
Zod or Valibot.

Both validate incoming data at runtime, which means they check real API values before your app trusts them.
TypeScript alone only checks your code while you build it.
It does not protect you from a broken JSON response at 2 a.m.

We tried Zod first.
It was easy to read, easy to debug, and our team moved fast.
But the validation layer started to feel heavy.
The bundle grew by 28 KB, and parsing large forms was noticeably slower in the browser.

Then we tested Valibot.
Smaller package, faster validation, less noise in the code.
On our forms, it cut validation time by about 40% and shaved enough bundle weight to matter on slower laptops.

We picked Valibot.

Not because Zod is bad.
Because for our use case, the lighter tool made the safety net cheap enough to keep on everywhere.

The lesson: if TypeScript is the guardrail, runtime validation is the seatbelt.
Pick the one your team will actually wear.

Which runtime validator would you trust in a production app?
