# LinkedIn Post

Topic: AI Evals

Date: 2026-09-07

---

Two evals, one model.

We were building an AI support assistant.
If it answered wrong, customers got angry.
If it answered slowly, they gave up.

So we had to decide what to measure first:
does the model sound good, or does it behave correctly?

That sounds small.
It isn’t.

A “good” answer can read nicely and still make up a refund policy.
A “correct” answer can be a little clunky and still save the ticket.

The tradeoff was simple:
human evals catch nuance, but they’re slow and expensive.
LLM-as-judge evals are fast, but they can miss the exact mistake that hurts a user.

We chose a hybrid.
First pass: GPT-4o as a judge, because it could score 500 outputs in minutes.
Second pass: a human reviewed only the failures that touched money, legal promises, or account access.

That constraint mattered more than elegance.
Our team was 4 people.
We couldn’t afford to label everything by hand every week.

The lesson:
when you evaluate AI, optimize for the mistake that costs you most, not the metric that looks smartest.

This tradeoff breaks down when the domain is tiny and every edge case is known.
Then human review can be the whole system.

What do you trust more for AI evals: a fast judge or a slower person?
