Evals saved our demo.

We were shipping an AI support assistant, and the question was simple:
which answers were actually good, and which ones just sounded confident?

I tried two tools for AI evals:
LangSmith, the tracing and test UI, and
OpenAI Evals, the prompt test harness.

LangSmith got us moving fast.
We could inspect 200 real conversations, tag failures, and see exactly where the assistant drifted.
Great for debugging.
Great for sharing with the team.

OpenAI Evals was lighter.
It let us score a prompt against a fixed set of cases in under an hour.
But once we wanted custom labels, side-by-side reviews, and messy real-world examples, it started feeling cramped.

We spent 3 days with both.

The winner was LangSmith.

Not because it was fancier.
Because evals only matter when they match the real product, not a clean benchmark.

That was the lesson:
the best eval tool is the one that makes bad behavior obvious to humans.

Which tool has made your AI mistakes impossible to ignore?
