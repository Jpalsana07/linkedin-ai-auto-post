One mask was off by 12 pixels.

I was building a vision feature for a production app that had to find a specific region in uploaded images.
If it missed, the whole workflow downstream would use the wrong crop.

I used SAM2, a segmentation model that draws object masks, and the output looked fine at a glance.
But one edge case kept failing.
The mask was shifted just enough that the next step cropped the wrong part of the image.

The problem was not the model.
It was my assumption that “looks close” was good enough.

I had treated computer vision like regular web input.
If a form field is 1 character off, you notice.
If a mask is 12 pixels off, the app can still look correct while quietly being wrong.

The fix was to validate the mask against the actual crop, not just the preview.
I added a simple check in FastAPI, and the bad cases started failing immediately instead of sneaking through.

The lesson: with vision systems, test the output your app uses, not the picture that looks nice.

What do you trust more in vision work: the model preview or the downstream check?
