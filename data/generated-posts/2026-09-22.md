# LinkedIn Post

Topic: Node.js at Scale

Date: 2026-09-22

---

We crashed checkout with one queue.

We were trying to handle a Black Friday traffic spike on a Node.js backend.
This was the path that took orders, charged cards, and sent confirmation emails.
When traffic jumped, one slow payment call blocked everything behind it.

The mistake was simple.
We treated Node like a machine that would just keep swallowing work.
Instead, one event loop got pinned, and a single retry storm turned into a 14-minute outage.

We found it in Datadog, the monitoring tool we used.
Latency climbed from 180ms to 11s, then errors started stacking up.
Customers were refreshing carts while our workers were stuck waiting on the same external API.

We fixed it by splitting the queue and putting hard timeouts on every payment request.
Nothing fancy.
Just fewer things allowed to wait on one another.

The lesson: at scale, Node.js fails less like a crash and more like a traffic jam.
