The launch page broke on day one.

We were building a simple signup flow for small businesses.
It was supposed to turn trial users into paying customers.
Instead, 38% of people dropped off before the first form loaded.

At first, I blamed the design.
Then I blamed the copy.
Then I spent two weeks moving buttons, trimming fields, and rewriting every headline.

Nothing moved.

The postmortem was painful because the bug was boring.
Our analytics script was blocking the page.
Analytics means the code that tracks visits and clicks.
One third-party tag from Segment, the customer data tool, was delaying the whole experience by 1.2 seconds.

We removed it from the critical path.
Critical path means the steps that must happen first.
Conversion jumped 19% the next week.

The lesson I keep coming back to:
if users disappear before they see your product, the problem is usually not your product.
