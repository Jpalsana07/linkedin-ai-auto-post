Postgres should stay boring.

The popular advice is to treat PostgreSQL like a temporary crate.
Use it early, then “graduate” to a bigger database when you scale.

We did the opposite.

Our app was handling 18 million rows, a few hundred writes a second, and a product team that kept shipping new filters every week.
Moving to a separate analytics store sounded smart.

Then we checked the numbers.

Postgres, with the right indexes and a few query fixes, kept our p95 latency under 140 ms.
The “faster” stack would have meant another system to run, another sync job to babysit, and another place for data to drift.

So we stayed.

Hot take: most teams do not outgrow Postgres.
They outgrow their habits.

The strongest counterargument is real:
if you need massive search, heavy cross-region traffic, or time-series data at absurd scale, Postgres may not be the whole answer.

But for a lot of products, the win is simpler than a new database.
Learn the one you already have.
Tune it.
Respect it.
Let it carry more than the internet says it can.
