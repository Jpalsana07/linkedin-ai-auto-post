# LinkedIn Post

Topic: LLM in Production

Date: 2026-10-10

---

We trusted the first answer.

We were rolling out an LLM assistant inside a support app.
If it worked, it would cut 200 repetitive tickets a day.
If it failed, real customers would get bad answers in front of our team.

The problem looked small at first.

The model sounded confident, so we shipped with a simple “answer only if sure” prompt.
But “sure” is not a feeling a chatbot actually has.
It kept guessing on refund policy, shipping delays, and account access.

One Monday, 14 answers in a row were wrong.

The fix was not a fancier model.
We added a retrieval step — pulling the answer from our help docs first — and made the bot quote the source before replying.
That extra guardrail cut hallucinations hard, but it also slowed responses a bit.

That tradeoff was worth it.

The lesson: in production, confidence is cheap.
Proof is what customers pay for.

Where have you seen “sounds right” fail harder than “is right”?
