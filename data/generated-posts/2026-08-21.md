# LinkedIn Post

Topic: SaaS Build-in-Public Metrics

Date: 2026-08-21

---

3 dashboards, still blind.

I was building a SaaS for founders who wanted to share build-in-public metrics without pulling numbers by hand.

The charts looked polished.

The problem was simple: nobody trusted them.

I spent 2 weeks wiring Stripe, PostHog, and Google Sheets into one dashboard.

Stripe was the payments source.
PostHog is product analytics.
Google Sheets was the manual fallback.

On paper, we had live revenue, signups, and activation.

In practice, one broken API rate limit — the limit on how often a service lets you ask — made the whole story drift by 18 hours.

A founder would post "we grew 14% today" and the dashboard would quietly show yesterday’s data.

That gap killed confidence.

The root cause wasn’t the integrations.

It was pretending all metrics had the same freshness.

Revenue can lag.
Product usage can update fast.
Manual inputs are even slower.

I rebuilt the product around freshness labels instead of one pretty number.

Each metric now says when it last updated.

The lesson: build-in-public metrics are not a design problem first.

They are a trust problem.

If the number can be stale, say so plainly.

What would you rather see first: a clean dashboard or an honest one?
