Topic: MCP and Context Engineering

Date: 2026-08-27

---

The bot knew too much.

We were building an internal AI helper for a support team of 42 people.

It could read tickets, draft replies, and fetch customer history.

Then it started pulling the wrong account details.

The scary part was that the model sounded confident every time.

We had wired in MCP, the Model Context Protocol, which is a standard way for an AI to ask for tools and data.

We also stuffed the prompt with everything we thought it might need.

That was the mistake.

The model didn’t fail because it was “not smart enough.”

It failed because we gave it a messy room and called it context.

We had mixed 11 tools, too many fields, and no clear order for what mattered first.

So the bot often grabbed the nearest answer, not the right one.

We fixed it by shrinking the tool list, naming each tool for one job, and sending only the customer data relevant to that ticket.

Latency dropped by 38%.

Wrong-account lookups went to zero in the next 2 weeks.

The lesson: in AI systems, more context is not safer context.

Clear context beats big context.

What’s the hardest part for you: choosing what the bot should know, or what it should ignore?
