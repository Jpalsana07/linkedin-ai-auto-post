Postgres is not your problem.

The hot take: your schema is.

Most teams blame PostgreSQL when queries get slow.
So they reach for bigger machines, more replicas, or a cache like Redis.

We did that too.

Our app was crawling on a table with 48 million rows.
The dashboard timeout was 8 seconds, and customers were noticing.

The fix wasn’t “better Postgres.”
It was one ugly, boring schema change.

We had packed five meanings into one text column.
That meant the database had to guess, scan, and sort too much.

We split the data, added one index, and removed a query that looked clever but was actually expensive.

Latency dropped from 8 seconds to 140 milliseconds.

That’s the part people miss:
PostgreSQL is usually fine.
The real cost is making the database clean up a messy model after your app ships it.

The strongest counterargument is fair:
sometimes Postgres really is the bottleneck, and then tuning, partitioning, or read replicas matter.

But if your tables are confused, no amount of hardware will save you.

The lesson: before blaming the database, ask whether your data model is making simple work hard.
