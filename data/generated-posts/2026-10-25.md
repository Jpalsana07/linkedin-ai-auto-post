# LinkedIn Post

Topic: LLM in Production

Date: 2026-10-25

---

Our support bot shipped.

It answers questions for e-commerce teams.
Last week I pushed a new LLM in production path so it could handle refunds, shipping, and order status without a human.

We shipped three things:
a stricter tool schema, the menu of actions the bot can take
better retrieval, the step that fetches the right policy text
and a fallback that hands off to a person when confidence is low

The good news: average reply time dropped from 9.4 seconds to 2.1.
Escalations to humans fell 18%.

The bad news: the bot still hallucinates on edge-case refund policies.
If a customer asks about a partial refund after a split shipment, it sometimes sounds certain and wrong.

That is the part people miss about LLMs in production.

The demo is not the hard part.
The hard part is deciding when the model should answer, when it should search, and when it should stop talking.

This week reminded me of one lesson:
a production LLM is not a brain.
It is a very fast employee that needs tight rules.

What’s the hardest thing you’d trust an LLM to do in production?
