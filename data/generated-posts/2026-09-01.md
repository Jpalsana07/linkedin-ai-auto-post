I chose boring over clever.

We were building a customer tool that had to stay up every day.
If it broke, real people couldn’t do their jobs.

The decision was simple in English:
one big service, or several smaller ones.

One big service is easier to understand.
Several smaller services are easier to change without touching everything.

The tradeoff is painful.
With microservices, you get cleaner boundaries, but you also get more deploys, more logs, and more ways for a tiny bug to become a long afternoon.

I chose the big service.

Not because it was elegant.
Because we were a 4-person team and every new system had a hidden tax:
more meetings, more monitoring, more “who owns this?”

The constraint most people miss is team shape.
At 4 people, speed came from fewer moving parts, not more isolation.

That choice saved us months.
It also meant saying no to some future-proofing we probably didn’t need yet.

This tradeoff matters less once a team is large enough that one person can own one service end to end.

When have you seen the “scalable” option slow a small team down?
