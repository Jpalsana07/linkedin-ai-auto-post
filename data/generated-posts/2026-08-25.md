Our evals finally caught it.

I’m building an AI support copilot for e-commerce teams.

This week I shipped the eval harness so we can test every prompt change before it reaches merchants.

Before that, we were guessing.
A reply could sound great in Slack and still fail on a refund, a shipping delay, or a product return.

I started with 200 real customer tickets.

Then I scored the model on three things:
correct answer, polite tone, and whether it asked for the right missing detail.

The good news: our pass rate moved from 71% to 84% after one prompt change and a tighter test set.

The bad news: it still misses edge cases when a ticket mentions two problems at once.

That matters because those are the tickets human agents usually dread most.

The lesson I keep relearning:
if you don’t measure the messy cases, you ship confidence instead of quality.

This week I also learned that “looks fine” is not a metric.

What would you trust more for AI quality: a clean demo or a test set built from the ugliest real tickets?
