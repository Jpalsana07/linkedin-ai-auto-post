We had two ways to run jobs.

One queue was BullMQ, the other was Temporal.
Both solve the same problem: what happens when work takes minutes, fails halfway, and needs to resume without losing data.

We hit this while building a payments sync service.
It had to retry failed imports, survive deploys, and not double-charge anyone.

BullMQ was quick to ship.
We had a working queue in 2 days.

But as soon as we needed branching steps, manual retries, and visibility into stuck jobs, it got messy.
We were stitching state together across Redis, logs, and our app.

Temporal was slower on day one.
It took us 8 days to get the first workflow right.

But after that, the difference was obvious.
A failed step could resume from the exact point it stopped.
The workflow history was visible in one place.
The code read like a checklist instead of a pile of callback glue.

The lesson: pick the tool that matches the cost of failure, not the speed of the first demo.

For simple background jobs, BullMQ is great.
For long-running business processes where mistakes are expensive, Temporal wins.

Which one would you trust for a payment flow?
