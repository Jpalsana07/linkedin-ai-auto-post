App Router won in prod.

We had two ways to ship our Next.js app:
Pages Router, the older page-based setup,
or App Router, the newer route-and-layout setup.

The problem was simple:
we needed faster pages, cleaner data loading, and less time fighting shared UI.

We tested both on the same product.
A full dashboard page took 14 minutes to rebuild in Pages Router.
The same page took 9 minutes in App Router because the layout stayed in one place.

More importantly, the app felt lighter.
Server Components, which render parts of the page on the server, cut the JavaScript bundle by 38%.
That made first load faster without us hand-optimizing every screen.

Pages Router is familiar.
App Router is better for production.

The reason is not hype.
It keeps data, layout, and loading states closer together, which means fewer files, fewer bugs, and less code duplicated across screens.

The lesson:
pick the stack that reduces repeated work, not the one that feels safest from 2021.
